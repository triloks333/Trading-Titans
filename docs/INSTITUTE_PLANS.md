# Trading Titans for Offline Stock Institutes

Commercial plan for selling Trading Titans as a practice platform to offline
stock-market coaching institutes. Three plans, what each includes, what each
costs, and what we still have to build before we can sell it.

Written as a commercial document, not a technical one. Prices are proposals, not
decisions.

---

## 1. Why institutes are the right channel right now

Trading Titans has roughly 78 real accounts today. On a free consumer app, 1–3%
of users convert to a paid subscription. That is one or two paying customers.

To earn ₹15,00,000 a year from consumers, at ₹299/month we would need about 570
subscribers, which at a 2% conversion rate means roughly **28,000 active users**
we do not have.

The same ₹15,00,000 comes from **18 institute contracts**.

| Route | Customers needed | Users needed | Sales effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer subscriptions | ~570 paying | ~28,000 active | Advertising spend |
| Institute contracts | 18 institutes | ~1,450 students | 18 conversations |

Three further advantages that matter more than the headline number:

**No Google Play fee.** A consumer subscription bought inside the Android app
carries a Play service fee that starts at 10% and applies to auto-renewing
subscriptions regardless of which billing system is used. An institute contract
is a direct B2B invoice, so none of that applies. We keep the whole amount.

**GST is not a cost.** Institutes with a GSTIN claim 18% GST as input credit, so
we quote B2B prices *exclusive* of GST and the tax is neutral to them. Consumer
prices have to be GST-inclusive, which quietly removes 15% of every rupee.

**Students become consumers.** A batch finishes in three or four months. Those
students already know the app, already have a portfolio and a login. The
institute contract is also our cheapest consumer acquisition channel.

## 2. What we can honestly sell today

This section exists so nobody promises a feature in a sales meeting that does not
exist. Everything in the left column works right now, in production.

### Working today

**Real-market paper trading**
- NSE and BSE equities at live prices
- US stocks (Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia and the rest)
- Cryptocurrency, priced in rupees from Indian venues
- Market and limit orders, with pending orders that expire at 3:40 PM IST like
  the real market
- Realistic charges applied on every trade — brokerage, STT, exchange fees,
  stamp duty, GST — configurable centrally

**Futures and options**
- Index and stock F&O with a full option chain, real strikes and open interest
- 15% margin blocked on futures and short options, 100% on equity and crypto
- Automatic expiry settlement on expiry day

**Portfolio and performance**
- Holdings, average price, unrealised and realised P&L
- Daily P&L that resets each session
- A full wallet statement: every credit, debit, reward and trade settlement with
  a running balance

**Learning and engagement**
- 25 challenges paying ₹200 to ₹50,000 in demo cash, with XP and Titan levels
- Weekly podium, awarded automatically each Sunday
- Trade Autopsy — a post-mortem of a closed trade explaining what went wrong
- What-If simulator — "if I had invested ₹X in Y, Z years ago"
- Trading glossary with a quiz that pays demo cash
- Price alerts and watchlists
- Leaderboard

**Access**
- Android app on Google Play
- Web app in any desktop browser (new — no install needed, works on institute
  lab machines)
- Guest mode for a zero-friction trial

### Does NOT exist yet

The app has no concept of an organisation. There are no batches, no faculty
accounts, no seats, and the leaderboard is global — every user in the country in
one list. All of the following has to be built before the corresponding plan can
be sold:

| Capability | Needed for | Size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Institute account + seat pool | All three plans | Medium |
| Join-by-code student onboarding | All three plans | Small |
| Private leaderboard scoped to the institute | All three plans | Small |
| Faculty read-only view of student activity | All three plans | Medium |
| Batches within an institute | Institute, Academy | Medium |
| Faculty-set starting capital per batch | Institute, Academy | Small |
| CSV export of student performance | Institute, Academy | Small |
| Multiple branches with rollup reporting | Academy | Medium |
| Institute logo and name shown in-app | Academy | Small |
| Completion certificates | Academy | Medium |

**Nothing can be sold before the first four rows are built.** The realistic
sequence is in section 9.

## 4. Pricing

All prices **exclusive of 18% GST**. Institutes reclaim GST as input credit, so
exclusive quoting is both standard and cheaper for them in real terms.

### Annual contract (recommended)

| Plan | Seats | Per seat / year | **Annual price** | Effective per seat / month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Classroom | 25 | ₹1,400 | **₹35,000** | ₹117 |
| Institute | 100 | ₹1,100 | **₹1,10,000** | ₹92 |
| Academy | 300 | ₹850 | **₹2,55,000** | ₹71 |

### Term contract (4 months — one batch cycle)

Coaching institutes do not think in years, they think in batches. A three or
four month course is the natural unit, and a term price closes deals an annual
price cannot.

| Plan | **Term price** | Per seat / term | Effective per seat / month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Classroom | **₹16,000** | ₹640 | ₹160 |
| Institute | **₹49,000** | ₹490 | ₹123 |
| Academy | **₹1,10,000** | ₹367 | ₹92 |

A term costs about 45% of the annual price for a third of the duration, so
annual remains clearly the better value — which is what we want.

### Extra seats

Charged at the plan's per-seat rate, pro-rated to the remaining contract period.
An institute on Classroom with 32 students pays for 7 extra seats, not an upgrade
to Institute. When extra seats push the total past the next plan's included
count, we upgrade them instead — the bigger plan is always cheaper per seat.

### How this compares to consumer pricing

Proposed consumer pricing is ₹299/month, or ₹2,499/year.

| Plan | Per seat / year | Discount vs consumer annual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classroom | ₹1,400 | 44% off |
| Institute | ₹1,100 | 56% off |
| Academy | ₹850 | 66% off |

The discount is real but it buys volume, zero marketing cost, no Play fee, and
annual cash up front instead of monthly churn.

### What we actually keep

| | Consumer ₹299/month | Classroom seat | Institute seat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| User pays | ₹299 | ₹117/month equivalent | ₹92/month equivalent |
| Less 18% GST | ₹253 | ₹117 (GST extra) | ₹92 (GST extra) |
| Less Play service fee ~10% | ₹223 | — | — |
| **Net to us** | **₹223** | **₹117** | **₹92** |

An institute seat priced at 39% of the consumer price returns 52% of the consumer
net revenue, and arrives 25 to 300 seats at a time.

## 5. The services, explained

### 5.1 Virtual trading capital

Each student receives their plan's virtual cash on joining: ₹20 lakh, ₹50 lakh or
₹1 crore. This is demo money. It has no cash value, cannot be withdrawn, and
cannot be transferred between students.

**Why the amounts differ by plan.** Bigger capital lets a student hold a
realistic multi-stock portfolio and take F&O positions where margin alone runs
into lakhs. At ₹20 lakh a student can build a diversified equity portfolio. At
₹50 lakh they can run F&O strategies alongside it. At ₹1 crore they can practise
position sizing at a scale that resembles managing real money.

**Faculty-set starting capital (Institute and Academy).** A teacher can pin every
student in a batch to the same figure — ₹5 lakh, say — so a contest is decided by
skill rather than by who joined with more. This is the single most requested thing
in any classroom competition and it is what makes the platform teachable.

**Expiry.** Virtual capital is tied to the contract. When a contract ends and is
not renewed, subscription capital is withdrawn. The exact rules are in section 7,
and they must be in the contract before we take any money.

### 5.2 Private leaderboard

Today the leaderboard is a single national list. A student in a 25-person batch
sees themselves at rank 4,000 and learns nothing.

Each institute gets its own leaderboard containing only its own students.
Institute and Academy also get per-batch boards, and Academy gets branch-versus-
branch, which is genuinely useful for a chain deciding which centre teaches best.

**Ranked by percentage return, not by absolute profit.** This matters. If ranking
used rupee profit, the student with the most capital would always win, and buying
capital would buy rank. Percentage return against starting capital means a
₹5 lakh student can beat a ₹1 crore student. The platform already stores each
account's starting capital for exactly this purpose.

### 5.3 Trade Autopsy

For any closed trade, an automated post-mortem: entry and exit against the day's
range, how long the position was held, what the charges cost, and where the
decision went wrong. This is the feature teachers will use most, because it turns
"you lost money" into a specific, reviewable lesson.

Unlimited on all three institute plans. It will be metered on the free consumer
tier, which makes it a genuine reason to be on an institute plan.

### 5.4 What-If simulator

"If you had put ₹1,00,000 into Infosys five years ago, you would have X." Useful
for teaching compounding and the cost of sitting out. Included on all plans.

### 5.5 Challenges, XP and rewards

25 challenges paying ₹200 to ₹50,000 in demo cash — first trade, a green day,
a 20% return, an active week, and so on. Students earn XP and climb Titan levels.

Institute plans raise the monthly challenge cash ceiling from ₹75,000 to
₹1,50,000, ₹3,00,000 or ₹5,00,000. This is the ideal institute perk: it costs us
nothing real, and it rewards practising rather than paying.

### 5.6 Faculty dashboard

What a teacher can see about their own students:

- Who has logged in, and when they last traded
- Number of trades, win rate, average holding period
- Realised and unrealised P&L, and percentage return
- Which challenges each student has completed
- Batch leaderboard
- Who has not placed a single trade — the students who need chasing

Institute and Academy add per-batch analytics and CSV export, so a centre can put
Trading Titans data into its own progress reports.

**Read-only, and consented.** Faculty cannot trade on a student's behalf, cannot
move their money, and cannot see anything outside their own institute. Students
are told at join time exactly what their teacher can see. See section 8.

### 5.7 Contests

A teacher schedules a contest over a date range, optionally pinning everyone to
the same starting capital, and the platform ranks participants by percentage
return with a live board. Classroom runs one at a time; Institute and Academy run
as many as they like.

This is what institutes will actually use for their monthly "trading competition"
and it is the feature most likely to close a sale.

### 5.8 Web app

Students use Trading Titans in any desktop browser with no install. This matters
specifically for offline institutes: lab machines are usually Windows desktops
where nobody can install an Android app, and asking every student to use their
own phone during class is a discipline problem.

Included on all three plans. We are **not** gating the web app — it is our
cheapest distribution channel and our only fee-free payment channel.

### 5.9 Certificates (Academy)

A dated completion certificate carrying the student's final return, number of
trades and Titan level, with both the institute's and Trading Titans' branding.
Institutes want these because students want something to show, and it puts our
name on a document the student keeps.

## 6. Revenue model

### A realistic first year

| Plan | Contracts | Annual each | Revenue | Seats |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Classroom | 10 | ₹35,000 | ₹3,50,000 | 250 |
| Institute | 6 | ₹1,10,000 | ₹6,60,000 | 600 |
| Academy | 2 | ₹2,55,000 | ₹5,10,000 | 600 |
| **Total** | **18** | | **₹15,20,000** | **1,450** |

Against ~28,000 consumer users for the same money.

### Marginal cost per seat

Near zero. A student is database rows and a handful of API calls. The real costs
are fixed: the Supabase plan, market data, and our time. That makes this a
high-margin business whose only variable enemy is churn.

### Renewal is the whole business

A first-year contract is a sale. A second-year renewal is the business. The
things that drive renewal, in order:

1. Did students actually use it? A dormant licence never renews.
2. Did faculty use the dashboard? If the teacher never opened it, we were a toy.
3. Did they run contests? Contests are the habit that makes the tool feel
   essential.

We should treat "percentage of seats active in the last 30 days" as the single
most important number per account, and intervene when it drops.

### Pilot pricing

The first three institutes should be free or heavily discounted for one term, in
exchange for a written case study, a testimonial, and permission to name them.
Trading Titans has no institutional references yet, and the fourth sale is far
easier with three names to point at.

## 7. Virtual capital expiry — must be settled before selling

"Capital expires on non-renewal" is one sentence hiding four situations. Getting
these wrong means refund demands and angry institutes.

**1. Open positions at contract end.** A student holds ₹18 lakh of stock when the
contract lapses. Proposal: positions funded from institute capital are closed at
the last available price, the institute capital balance is zeroed, and the student
keeps a read-only record of their trade history.

**2. Profits earned on institute capital.** A student turns ₹20 lakh into ₹26
lakh. Proposal: the ₹6 lakh gain belongs to the institute capital bucket and goes
with it. What the student keeps is their history, statistics and certificate.

**3. The student's own money.** A student may have had ₹11,000 of free starting
cash, or bought a ₹999 top-up personally, before joining a batch. Proposal: this
is a separate, visibly-labelled balance that is never touched by contract expiry.
When the institute capital is withdrawn, the student drops back to their own
balance and continues as an ordinary consumer user. **This is also how we convert
students into consumer subscribers.**

**4. Late renewal.** An institute paying two weeks late is not a cancellation.
Proposal: 30-day grace period for institutes, during which nothing is withdrawn
and faculty get renewal reminders.

**Seat reassignment.** A student who leaves frees their seat after 30 days, and it
can be given to a new student. The departing student keeps their account, drops to
their own balance, and becomes a consumer user. Seats are not transferable
mid-batch to prevent one licence being cycled through hundreds of students.

## 8. Privacy, and what we must be careful about

Student trading data is personal. Today the database deliberately prevents any
user from reading another user's profile. Letting faculty see student performance
is a deliberate exception and has to be built as one, not bolted on.

Rules we should commit to in the contract:

- Faculty see students **in their own institute only**, never anyone else
- Faculty access is **read-only**: no trading, no moving money, no changing
  passwords, no deleting accounts
- Students are shown, at join time, exactly what their institute can see, and
  they must accept it before joining
- A student who leaves stops being visible to that institute
- We never sell or share student data with anyone else
- A student can request deletion of their account regardless of the contract

Two more commercial cautions:

**We are not an advisory service.** We must not let institutes present Trading
Titans as investment advice or as a signal service. The Terms already say the
platform is education with virtual money and no real trading. Institute contracts
must repeat it, because an institute mis-selling us creates a regulatory problem
that lands on us.

**No real money, ever.** No plan may allow real funds, real brokerage
integration, or conversion of virtual gains into anything of value. That line is
what keeps Trading Titans out of SEBI's remit, and it is not worth blurring for
any contract size.

## 9. Rollout sequence

**Phase 1 — before any sale (build)**
Institute account and seat pool, join-by-code onboarding, institute-scoped
private leaderboard, basic faculty roster view. Switch the leaderboard to
percentage-based ranking. Without these there is no product to sell.

**Phase 2 — sell Classroom only**
One plan, one price, term contracts. Target 3 pilot institutes at a discount for
case studies. Learn what faculty actually open and what students actually do.

**Phase 3 — build for Institute**
Batches, faculty-set starting capital, per-batch analytics, CSV export, contest
scheduling. Driven by what phase 2 taught us, not by this document.

**Phase 4 — sell Institute, launch consumer subscriptions**
By now the paid features exist and are proven in classrooms. Consumer tiers reuse
the same feature gates.

**Phase 5 — Academy**
Branches, rollup reporting, co-branding, certificates. Only worth building once a
chain has actually asked and is ready to sign.

Deliberately: **Academy should be quoted but not built** until a chain commits.
It is the plan most likely to be bought by exactly one customer with bespoke
requirements, and building it speculatively is the most expensive mistake
available here.

## 10. Open decisions

1. Are the seat counts right? 25 / 100 / 300 is a guess at Indian coaching batch
   sizes. Real numbers from two or three institutes would beat my assumption.
2. Term length: 4 months, or 3? Should match how their courses are actually sold.
3. Should Classroom get contests? It is the feature most likely to close the sale,
   which argues for including it even at the entry price.
4. Do we allow an institute to buy top-ups for students on top of the plan?
5. Who invoices — Trading Titans directly, or KWM? Affects GST treatment and the
   contract paper.
6. Payment terms: full amount up front, or 50/50 split across the term? Up front
   is better for us; split closes more deals.
7. Revenue share as an alternative: some institutes will prefer paying per
   enrolled student per month rather than a licence. Worth having a number ready.
8. Renewal discount for a second year — worth offering, and how much.
